# The story world: two shapes (PROPOSAL, for Michael)

**Status:** RULED 2026-10-08: Shape B, with cameos that link back to each character's home course
(Michael, via basecamp #5221). The next step is docs/proposals/lore-and-assessment.md. Nothing here is
written into a lesson until he chooses.

**His direction** (relayed verbatim by basecamp, #5210): "maybe an adventure track like fantasy to
sci-fi as they learn more skills and eventually building rockets, and the most advanced courses are
the team working on warp drive ... I am torn between having one set of characters working through
each in each age, like life of Fred, or new characters for each course."

**What holds in either shape:**
- Every character, place and event is invented here. Nothing is taken from the algebra book he named
  as inspiration: no ruler being taught, no court of advisors, no kingdom of problems brought to a
  throne. Nothing in it is about any real child.
- The story carries the examples; it never carries the mathematics. The prerequisite graph decides
  what a lesson leans on, and a learner who skips the story loses no maths.
- Every number in a story is one the calculator computes, through the vectors, as now.
- The top of the ladder is a warp-drive team. shipwright's bridge game already has a warp-drive world,
  the Emberline. The advanced courses could share it if he chooses, so neither shape invents a canon
  for that end yet.

The courses, young end to top, and where each sits in the arc:

| Course | Era of the world | What the maths builds |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-algebra (start-01, P1 to P10) | a fantasy market town | trade, recipes, sharing, a harvest that must last |
| Geometry (G1 to G9) | the age of exploration | maps, a bridge, a lighthouse, the stars for navigation |
| Algebra to Calculus (units 0 to 12) | the age of invention | engines, then the first rocket: rates, growth, trajectories |
| Advanced (not yet planned) | the warp age | the warp-drive team (the Emberline, if he chooses) |

## Shape A: one cast that grows up through the courses

Three friends from a hill town, 11 or 12 when pre-algebra begins:
- **Ossie** keeps the ledgers and counts everything.
- **Pell** builds first and measures second.
- **Juno** asks why.

They find a shuttered workshop whose last owner, an inventor long gone, left a shelf of notebooks.
The notebooks are the lessons. The friends work through them as the learner does. Each course is
the next part of their lives:
- **Pre-algebra:** they run a market stall to keep the workshop's rent paid. That means fractions of
  pies, a fair price, a harvest stored for winter.
- **Geometry, about 14:** they join a survey of the valley, bridge the river and chart the coast.
- **Algebra to Calculus, about 17:** the workshop becomes a foundry. Engines lead to the valley's
  first rocket.
- **The advanced courses:** grown, they are three engineers on the warp-drive team.

The world moves from fantasy to science fiction because they move it. Each era's marvel is something
the cast built from the maths before.

**How a lesson's maths drives the plot.** Each unit is one decision the cast must get right, and the
lesson's examples are their working:
- Is there grain enough for winter? That is fractions and rates.
- At what angle must the bridge's cable run? That is trigonometry.
- How much fuel lifts the rocket? That is the integral.
A wrong answer would cost them something in the story. The learner checks the answer.

**What it costs the lessons:**
- **Continuity is a second dependency graph that nothing checks.** A lesson's story leans on earlier
  story: who broke the mill wheel, why Pell is not speaking to Juno. Reordering units, adding a lesson,
  or writing two courses at once all risk the plot.
- **Re-introduction, every course.** A learner placed mid-ladder meets three strangers with a
  history. Each course needs a recap opening, and every first lesson of a unit needs one too, since
  placement lands learners at unit starts.
- **Ages and eras.** The cast cannot age decades as fast as a learner moves through four courses
  without the world jumping between them. Either the gaps are written in, or the cast stops
  matching the learner's age, which was most of the point.
- **One voice across 100 or more lessons.** That is a heavy constraint on several writers (tutor,
  and any later seat), and on the readers who check it.
- **The top is bent toward the cast.** If the warp end joins the Emberline, the friends must arrive in
  a canon written without them.

## Shape B: one world, a new crew for each course

The same world and its history, but each course follows new people in that course's era.
- **Pre-algebra: the market at Thornwick.** A baker's stall and the courier who runs its orders. The
  bakery examples already written fit here: start-01 and frac-01 would take names and little else.
- **Geometry: the survey ship.** Its youngest crew chart a coast and fix their place by the stars.
- **Algebra to Calculus: the rocket yard.** A club of apprentices builds an engine, then a rocket.
- **The advanced courses: the warp-drive team.** This is where the Emberline could join, if he
  chooses.

**What carries between courses.** The world's history and its objects, not its people:
- Thornwick is a port on the survey ship's charts.
- The survey's lighthouse becomes the rocket yard's tracking station.
- The baker's ledger, a ship's log and a rocket notebook pass from era to era as heirlooms.

A learner who did the earlier course recognises them. One who did not loses nothing. Each course's
heroes become the next course's history: a statue in the square, a name on a chart.

**How a lesson's maths drives the plot.** The same as in A, within one course: each unit is a decision
the crew must get right, and the examples are their working. Within a course the crew is constant,
so a learner reading in order gets the attachment A offers.

**What it costs the lessons:**
- **Less attachment across courses.** A learner who loves Thornwick's baker does not see her again,
  except as history.
- **A setup at the start of each course.** It is short, because the cast is new to everyone.
- **The fantasy-to-science-fiction arc lives in the world, not in a cast.** It has to be shown: what
  each era built is visible in the next.

## Recommendation: Shape B, one world with a new crew per course

The deciding reason is his other ask, placement. A pre-assessment that lands learners at the right
course and unit makes starting mid-ladder normal. Shape A charges for that at every course and every
unit start. Shape B charges nothing.

B also keeps each course free to be written, reordered or extended on its own. It lets the warp-drive
end join the Emberline without bending a cast. It keeps a crew's ages fitted to each course's
learners.

What A does best, a cast to grow up with, B keeps inside each course, where lessons are read in
order. The heirlooms and places carry a thread for a learner who climbs the whole ladder.

## If his children are the first learners

The kid-designer rule holds: a child's design is the spec. If they want to name the crew, draw the
town or choose what the rocket yard builds, their sheet decides. A missing value is a question for
them; where one is needed to proceed, a clearly flagged stand-in is used and the question goes back
in the same breath.

Two things are his to rule before any of their design enters a public CC BY-SA course: consent, and
credit. Names never publish.
