# Geometry with the STU-32 (PROPOSED syllabus)

**Status:** a proposal for Michael to push against (abacus decision 60, 2026-10-07: "pre-algebra and
geometry", for a first learner in geometry). Not ruled. His art-based trigonometry course is to be
folded in (asked for through abacus); the construction and drawing thread below is where it would
most naturally join.

**Scope sources, scope and order only:** the usual order of a first geometry course, and Euclid's
Elements, Books I to VI (books/Euclid, Casey's 1885 edition, public domain), for the method:
definitions, then postulates, then each step justified by a rule already stated. Nothing is taken
from a geometry textbook.

## The shape

- **Draw, measure, then prove.** Each idea starts with a drawing the learner makes (compass, ruler,
  protractor, or squared paper), then a measurement the calculator works with, then the reason it
  must be so. Proof grows out of "why does this always work?", in Euclid's manner: say what the
  words mean, say what is assumed, and justify every step.
- **The calculator's part** is the measuring and checking: π to 34 digits, square roots, the
  angle functions (trig-01..04, required where used), formulas as programs or as SOLVE equations
  (eq-02), and →POL and →REC for turning a point about the origin.
- **Stories and art** for the simpler topics (decision 60): a map to draw, a garden to lay out, a
  pattern to tile. Invented characters only.
- **The graph links back** to the algebra course where geometry leans on it (Pythagoras in trig-02,
  slope in lin-01, the unit circle in trig-03) and forward where geometry teaches what that course
  assumed (angles, similar triangles).

## Units

| # | Unit | The mathematics | On the STU-32 |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | Points, lines and angles | definitions; measuring angles in degrees; angle pairs (supplementary, vertical); parallel lines cut by a transversal | DEG (trig-01's ∡MODE); arithmetic on angles |
| G2 | Triangles | the angle sum; kinds of triangle; congruence (SSS, SAS, ASA); the isosceles triangle | checking an angle sum; Euclid's first construction, the equilateral triangle, drawn |
| G3 | Pythagoras and distance | the theorem and why it holds (a picture proof); its converse; distance between two points | √x; a distance program; trig-02's converse, required |
| G4 | Area | rectangles, parallelograms, triangles, trapezoids; composite shapes | formulas typed as equations and solved for any letter (eq-02) |
| G5 | Circles | circumference and area; arcs and sectors; angles in a circle | the π key; arc length (trig-01) |
| G6 | Similarity and scale | ratios of sides; similar triangles; scale drawings and maps | the bridge to trig-02's ratios |
| G7 | Solids | volume and surface area: prisms, cylinders, pyramids, cones, spheres | a formula as a program; SOLVE for a missing dimension |
| G8 | Coordinates and transformations | midpoint, slope (lin-01), translation, reflection, rotation | →POL and →REC to rotate a point about the origin |
| G9 | Proof | Euclid's way: definitions, postulates, a chain of reasons; proving the angle sum and Pythagoras | the calculator as a doubter: a measurement suggests, a proof settles |

About 27 lessons.
